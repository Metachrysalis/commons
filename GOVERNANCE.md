# Metachrysalis Commons Governance

## 1. Membership and Governance

The Metachrysalis Commons distinguishes between **Participating Members** and **Voting Members**.

Players Commons and Creators Commons members are Participating Members.

Participating Membership provides the rights, responsibilities, participation opportunities, and benefits established by the applicable Commons.

Participating Membership does not, by itself, confer a formal vote.

**Voting Membership** is a separate governance status. Voting Members are responsible for formal decisions concerning the official Metachrysalis Commons.

Organizations cannot be Voting Members. Voting Membership is held by individuals.

## 2. Participating Members

Participating Members are encouraged to participate meaningfully in the development of the Commons.

Depending on the Commons to which they belong, Participating Members may:

* exercise applicable Commons rights;
* contribute Commons Material;
* participate in Creator Participation and compensation where applicable;
* submit proposals;
* participate in discussions and consultations;
* raise concerns and objections;
* recommend policy changes;
* participate in working groups, circles, and other collaborative processes;
* advise Voting Members; and
* otherwise contribute to the development and stewardship of the Commons.

Voting Members should seek meaningful input from affected Participating Members when practical and appropriate.

Participation, consultation, consensus-seeking, or contribution does not itself create formal Voting Membership.

## 3. Voting Members

Voting Members have all rights available to them as Participating Members and additionally have the right and responsibility to vote on formal governance decisions.

Unless otherwise expressly provided, each Voting Member has one vote.

Voting Membership does not create ownership of Metachrysalis, its trademarks, repositories, or the contributions of other members.

## 4. Founding Voting Member

Metachrysalis has one **Founding Voting Member**.

The Founding Voting Member's status:

* is permanent;
* has no fixed term;
* does not require renewal;
* does not expire through inactivity;
* cannot be revoked or suspended by another Voting Member, Participating Member, committee, working group, delegate, or other body established under this governance framework; and
* may not be removed or materially diminished through an amendment to this governance framework without the Founding Voting Member's express consent.

The Founding Voting Member's status ends only through:

* voluntary relinquishment by the Founding Voting Member;
* death; or
* implementation of a successor governance arrangement expressly established or accepted by the Founding Voting Member.

## 5. Appointed Voting Members

The Founding Voting Member may appoint a Participating Member as an **Appointed Voting Member**.

Each appointment is for a term of **one year** beginning on the effective date stated in the appointment.

An Appointed Voting Membership:

* expires automatically at the end of its term;
* may be renewed through a new affirmative appointment;
* creates no entitlement or expectation of renewal;
* does not become permanent through repeated renewal; and
* does not create any right of succession to the Founding Voting Membership.

Appointment and renewal must be recorded in `VOTING-MEMBERS.md`.

## 6. Protection of an Appointed Voting Member's Term

An Appointed Voting Member may not be removed merely because:

* they disagree with the Founding Voting Member;
* they vote against a proposal;
* they advocate a minority position;
* they criticize a Commons decision in good faith; or
* their continued participation has become politically or personally inconvenient.

Once appointed, an Appointed Voting Member is entitled to serve the remainder of their term except as provided below.

## 7. Early End of an Appointed Voting Membership

An Appointed Voting Membership may end before its expiry only through:

1. voluntary resignation;
2. death or incapacity preventing continued service;
3. legal inability to serve;
4. loss of eligibility expressly required for Voting Membership; or
5. removal for cause.

### Removal for Cause

Cause may include:

* material breach of a Commons Agreement;
* material breach of the duties of Voting Membership;
* fraud or deliberate material misrepresentation relating to Commons affairs;
* serious misuse of governance authority;
* serious misconduct creating a material risk to the Commons or its members; or
* another comparably serious breach of trust or responsibility.

Good-faith disagreement, dissent, criticism, or an adverse vote does not constitute cause.

Before removal for cause:

* the grounds must be stated in writing;
* the affected Voting Member must receive a reasonable opportunity to respond;
* the decision must be made in good faith on the documented grounds; and
* all Voting Members other than the affected member must approve the removal.

The affected Voting Member does not vote on their own removal.

The Founding Voting Member's assent is required for any removal decision.

A record of the decision must be retained, although confidential or sensitive information need not be made public.

## 8. Formal Decisions

While there is only one Voting Member, that Voting Member may make formal decisions without convening a meeting or recording a formal ballot.

When there is more than one Voting Member:

1. a decision ordinarily requires a simple majority of votes cast at a quorate decision; and
2. the decision must receive the affirmative vote or express assent of the Founding Voting Member.

Both requirements must be satisfied.

### Quorum

A quorum consists of a majority of current Voting Members and must include the Founding Voting Member.

Voting may occur synchronously or asynchronously, including through an appropriately documented GitHub issue, pull request, decision record, meeting, or other governance process.

## 9. Matters Subject to Formal Governance

Formal governance includes decisions concerning:

* admission, suspension, and removal of Participating Members;
* Commons Agreements;
* governance rules;
* contribution standards;
* Creator Participation and compensation policy;
* work-registration policy;
* Sustaining Organization License approvals;
* organizational fee and waiver policy;
* official interpretations of Commons policy;
* creation of additional official Metachrysalis Commons;
* official Metachrysalis releases;
* official repositories and publication channels;
* delegation of governance authority;
* official use of Metachrysalis identity and marks; and
* amendments to this governance framework.

Routine administrative functions may be delegated.

## 10. Delegation

Voting Members may delegate defined administrative or decision-making authority to an individual, working group, circle, committee, or other body.

A delegation should identify:

* the authority delegated;
* its scope;
* applicable limits;
* its duration, where appropriate; and
* how it may be withdrawn.

Delegated authority does not confer Voting Membership.

Delegation may be amended or withdrawn through a formal governance decision.

## 11. Consultation and Consensus

Formal voting is a mechanism for making decisions, not a substitute for collaborative governance.

Voting Members are encouraged to seek discussion, consent, consensus, consultation, and advice before significant decisions where doing so is practical.

The Commons may use:

* proposals;
* discussions;
* consent processes;
* polls;
* working groups;
* advisory circles;
* community consultations; and
* other participatory methods.

Unless expressly designated otherwise in advance, such processes inform formal governance but do not replace the vote required by this document.

## 12. Conflicts of Interest

Voting Members must disclose material conflicts of interest relevant to decisions before them.

An Appointed Voting Member may recuse themselves where appropriate.

Additional conflict-of-interest procedures may be established through governance policy.

## 13. Rights Independent of Governance

Governance authority does not give Voting Members ownership of other members' contributions.

Governance decisions may not retroactively erase:

* copyright ownership;
* compensation already earned;
* attribution rights;
* lawful acts already completed;
* rights that expressly survive under an existing license or agreement; or
* other vested rights that governance does not have lawful authority to revoke.

Policy changes ordinarily operate prospectively.

## 14. Amendments

This governance framework may be amended through the formal decision process.

No amendment may:

* remove the Founding Voting Member;
* place a term limit on the Founding Voting Membership;
* make the Founding Voting Membership revocable;
* eliminate the requirement for the Founding Voting Member's assent; or
* materially diminish the protected governance rights of the Founding Voting Member

without the Founding Voting Member's express consent.

## 15. Succession

The Founding Voting Member may establish a successor or successor governance process.

No person acquires succession rights merely through:

* Participating Membership;
* Appointed Voting Membership;
* repeated appointment;
* employment;
* contribution;
* financial support;
* repository access; or
* length of service.

## 16. Independent Commons

Communities using the Metachrysalis Commons framework to establish their own Commons may adopt this governance model, modify it, or use an entirely different governance structure.

Their governance does not become part of official Metachrysalis governance merely because they use Metachrysalis templates or Commons Material.
